About this model

A closed-loop circuit model: dI/dt = −(R/L)I. The sample has zero resistance below an illustrative Tc = 90 K; above it, its decay time is the same as the reference. The resistive time constant is 2.5 demonstration seconds. Cooling alone does not create or restore a current. Arrows show conventional current, not individual electron trajectories. The reference is assumed resistive throughout; this is not a microscopic model of pairing.

Best use: extend or replace the existing temperature opening. Visitors see the consequence of R = 0, beyond moving a point on a graph.

About this model

An ideal diamagnetic cylinder in a transverse uniform field, with zero field inside in the Meissner state. Field lines outside are contours of y[1 − a²/(x² + y²)]. Line density represents relative field strength; every nonzero setting is assumed safely within the weak-field Meissner regime. Penetration depth, intermediate states, vortices and trapped flux are omitted. This diagram does not model stable magnetic levitation.

Best use: beside the Meissner-effect explanation and levitating-magnet illustration. Cooling in an existing field makes the expulsion visible.

About this model

A simplified type-I phase boundary Hc(T) / Hc(0) = 1 − [T / Tc(0)]² for T < Tc(0), treating applied field as uniform and neglecting demagnetisation, hysteresis and intermediate states. Axes are normalised; no specific material is predicted. Cuprates and nickelates are type-II materials, with additional field regimes. This map introduces the idea of a phase boundary, not their phase diagrams.

Best use: an optional deeper section after the basic properties. This is richer than the opening temperature slider, but less directly connected to the charge-order story.

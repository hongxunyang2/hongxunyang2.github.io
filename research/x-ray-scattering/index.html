01 / The idea in a minute

Tiny worlds.
Bright clues.

How do you look inside a crystal without taking it apart? Send in X-rays and study what comes back.

Where the X-rays go tells us about the arrangement of atoms. Measuring how much energy they lose can reveal motion inside the material.

We use these clues to study superconducting material families: how their atoms vibrate, how their magnetic moments move, and how their electrons organise.

Diffraction
Use the pattern of scattered X-rays to learn about crystal structure.
RIXS
Resonant inelastic X-ray scattering: tune the incoming X-rays and measure the energy they exchange with a material.
Why superconductors? Below a certain temperature, they carry DC electrical current with zero resistance. Meet these remarkable materials ↗

03 / Your possible project

Turn a pattern into an answer.

Choose a focus: data, crystal structure or the experiment itself.

Three starting points

IDEA / 01

Find a hidden signal.

Work with RIXS spectra. Separate overlapping peaks and see how a vibration or magnetic signal changes with temperature or angle.

Data analysis · Python
Bachelor’s starting point

Analyse a small existing dataset, fit selected peaks and check how uncertain the results are.

Possible Master’s extension

Compare a larger set of measurements and test a physical model; beamtime participation may be possible.

IDEA / 02

Rebuild a crystal’s structure.

Use X-ray diffraction patterns to work out where atoms sit. This process is called structure refinement.

Diffraction · Structure refinement
Bachelor’s starting point

Refine a known crystal structure from existing data and assess how well the model matches it.

Possible Master’s extension

Investigate subtle structural changes with temperature or strain, and compare competing structure models.

IDEA / 03

Join an X-ray experiment.

Help plan measurements, prepare samples and turn detector images into useful scientific plots.

Experiments · Teamwork
Bachelor’s starting point

Prepare and analyse one part of an experiment, with a focused question and an existing-data fallback.

Possible Master’s extension

Take a larger role in an experimental campaign, subject to approved beamtime and the project schedule.

These ideas can be adapted to a Bachelor’s or Master’s thesis. We’ll choose a specific material, dataset and achievable question together.

04 / A question from our research

One spectrum. Several stories.

Which part is the vibration?

Several processes can overlap in one RIXS spectrum. In our copper-oxide study, modelling the atomic vibrations helped uncover a weaker signal from collective charge motion.

You don’t need to understand every label here. The starting question is simple: which parts of the data come from which processes?
